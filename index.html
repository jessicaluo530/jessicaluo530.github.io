02Research

Working Papers

Position-Driven Allocation by LLM Recommenders

with Stefanus Jasin, Jane Jiang, and Zijie Zhou

Working paperLLMsRecommender systemsChoice modeling

When large language models recommend products, where an item appears in the list can matter as much as what it is. We treat LLM position bias as an allocation mechanism: using a multinomial-logit framework, we show when recommenders lean on position rather than attributes, and run controlled experiments that measure how position reliance shifts demand — and consumer value — across sellers.

Having It All? Childcare Market Entry and the Geography of Childcare Deserts

with Jun Li and coauthors

Working paperMarket entryPublic policySpatial data

Why do childcare deserts form, and where? We develop a two-stage entry and pricing model that links parents' labor supply to providers' staffing costs, calibrate it to U.S. census and establishment data, and map childcare supply across rural, suburban, and urban markets. The model suggests subsidy design should differ by geography — supporting families in rural areas and providers in dense cities.

Teacher Incentives and Student Learning: A Field Experiment in Rwanda

with Mahyar Eftekhar and Jun Li · Arizona State University & University of Michigan, in partnership with NABU

Field experimentIncentive designEducationINFORMS Annual Meeting 2026

A randomized evaluation comparing a collective class-fund incentive with an individual teacher bonus across primary schools in Rwanda. I lead data cleaning and analysis, partner coordination in the field, and the design of the teacher survey used to study mechanisms.

Information Asymmetry in U.S. Childcare Markets

with Jun Li

In progressQuality ratingsInformation design

How do quality rating and improvement systems (QRIS) and subsidy rules shape what parents can learn about providers? We assemble a 50-state database of QRIS and subsidy policies to study how information gaps affect childcare choice and market outcomes.
